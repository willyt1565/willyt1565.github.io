Hidden Fees to Watch for in a Property Management Contract

The monthly percentage is only one part of what a property manager costs you. The fees that really add up are often further into the contract: maintenance markups, admin and technology fees, vacancy fees, inspection charges, renewal fees and termination penalties. Before you sign anything, ask for a complete fee schedule in writing and add up a realistic year. Here are the fees to look for in any Texas management agreement, and the question to ask about each one.

In short

  • The lowest monthly percentage isn't always the lowest total cost.
  • Get every fee in writing, including repairs, inspections, renewals and termination.
  • Confirm the company holds a Texas real estate broker license before you sign.

1. Maintenance markups

Some managers add a percentage, often around 10%, to every vendor invoice. Others own an in-house maintenance company and bill you their own rates. Neither is automatically wrong, but you should know it's happening.

Ask: "Do you add anything to vendor invoices, and will I see the original invoice?"

At Newera, there is no markup on vendor invoices. You pay what the vendor charges.

2. Administration, technology or portal fees

These are monthly or annual charges for software, "processing" or owner portal access. They look small on their own, but they add up over a year, and faster across several homes.

Ask: "Are there any recurring fees besides the management percentage?"

3. Fees on rent due instead of rent collected

If the management fee is based on rent due, you pay it even when the tenant doesn't pay you. Your contract should spell out which one applies.

Ask: "Is your fee calculated on rent collected?"

4. Vacancy fees

A monthly charge while the home sits empty. Beyond the cost, it removes some of the manager's incentive to lease the home quickly.

Ask: "Do you charge anything while the home is vacant?"

5. Leasing fees and re-leasing fees

Most companies charge a leasing fee to find and place a tenant, commonly a share of the first month's rent. Check whether the same fee applies every time a tenant turns over, and whether the company offers any guarantee if a tenant they placed leaves early. For reference, Newera's leasing fee is typically 50% of the first month's rent when we place a new resident.

6. Renewal fees

Some companies charge a flat amount, others a percentage of one month's rent, each time a tenant renews. Ask what's included, such as a market rent review and a new lease.

7. Inspection fees

Some companies charge separately for move-in, routine or move-out inspections.

Ask: "How often do you inspect, and is it included in the management fee?"

Newera inspects every property we manage every six months and documents each visit with photos.

8. Setup or onboarding fees

A one-time charge to add your property to the company's system. Find out exactly what it covers.

9. Eviction and court coordination fees

These are separate from court filing fees and attorney costs. Ask what the manager charges for their own time if an eviction becomes necessary. Texas eviction procedures were updated on January 1, 2026, when Senate Bill 38 took effect, so it's also fair to ask how the company handles the current process.

10. Early termination fees

What does it cost to leave if the relationship isn't working? Some contracts lock you in for a year or charge the remaining fees for the lease term. Read this clause before you sign, not when you want out.

11. Fees on extra services

Look for separate charges for things like insurance claim handling, HOA violation responses, project management on large repairs, court appearances or annual tax documents.

Other contract terms to read closely

  • Repair approval limit. The dollar amount the manager can spend without asking you first. Make sure it's a number you're comfortable with.
  • Reserve requirement. How much of your money the manager holds back for repairs.
  • Who owns the tenant relationship. Some contracts say the manager is still owed fees if you keep a tenant they placed after you leave.
  • Payout timing. When you get paid each month.
  • Licensing. In Texas, a company that leases and manages property for others generally needs a real estate broker license. You can look up any company on the Texas Real Estate Commission website. Newera's broker license is #9016567-BB.

How to compare two contracts

Build a simple one-year estimate for each company using the same assumptions: your rent, one tenant renewal, about $2,000 to $3,000 in repairs and two inspections. Then add every fee. The company with the lowest percentage is often not the cheapest once everything is counted.

Tip: Ask each company to fill in the same one-year estimate for your home. A company that's confident in its pricing won't mind putting the numbers in writing.

Frequently asked questions

Is a maintenance markup always bad?

Not necessarily, as long as it's disclosed and you're getting fair vendor pricing. The problem is a markup you don't know about.

What's a normal property management fee in Texas?

Many full-service managers in Texas charge roughly 8% to 12% of the monthly rent, plus a leasing fee when they place a new tenant. Newera's management fee is typically 8%. For a closer look at local pricing, see How Much Does Property Management Cost in San Antonio?

Can I negotiate a property management contract?

Often, yes, especially if you own several properties. Newera offers portfolio discounts for owners with multiple homes, down to as low as 6%.

How do I check if a property manager is licensed in Texas?

Search the company or broker name on the Texas Real Estate Commission (TREC) website. A licensed brokerage has an active broker license number. Newera's is #9016567-BB.
