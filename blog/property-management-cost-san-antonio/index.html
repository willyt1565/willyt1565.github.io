How Much Does Property Management Cost in San Antonio?

By the Newera teamNovember 9, 20265 min read
On this page
  1. The fees you'll see on most quotes
  2. The fees that don't show up in the headline number
  3. A quick way to compare two quotes
  4. What you should get for the fee
  5. Is property management worth the cost?
  6. Frequently asked questions

Most San Antonio property managers charge a monthly management fee of roughly 8% to 12% of the rent collected, plus a leasing fee when they place a new tenant, often around half of the first month's rent. At Newera, our management fee is typically 8% of the monthly rent, and owners with several homes can qualify for portfolio discounts down to as low as 6%. Our leasing fee is typically 50% of the first month's rent, we don't mark up vendor invoices, and renewal fees are quoted during a free consultation. The percentage is only part of the picture, though. Two companies can both advertise "8%" and cost you very different amounts by the end of the year. Here's how to read a San Antonio management quote and compare it fairly.

In short

  • Expect a monthly fee of roughly 8% to 12% of the rent, plus a leasing fee.
  • Compare the full yearly cost, not the headline percentage.
  • Make sure the fee is charged on rent collected and every extra fee is in writing.

The fees you'll see on most quotes

  • Monthly management fee. A percentage of the rent collected each month. Most percentage fees in Texas fall roughly between 8% and 12%, depending on the service level and how many homes you own. Make sure the fee is charged on rent collected, not rent due. If a tenant doesn't pay, you shouldn't pay a fee on money you never received.
  • Leasing or tenant placement fee. Charged when the manager finds and places a new tenant. In San Antonio this is commonly around 50% of the first month's rent, and some companies charge more.
  • Lease renewal fee. Charged when an existing tenant signs a new lease. Some companies charge a flat fee; others charge a percentage of one month's rent.
  • Setup or onboarding fee. A one-time charge some companies use to bring a new property into their system.

The fees that don't show up in the headline number

This is where quotes get hard to compare. Ask every company you interview whether they charge any of these. For a full checklist, see Hidden Fees to Watch for in a Property Management Contract.

  • Maintenance markups. Some managers add 10% or more on top of every vendor invoice. On a $4,000 HVAC replacement, that's an extra $400 you may never see itemized. Newera does not mark up vendor invoices.
  • Administration or technology fees. Monthly or annual charges for "portal access," "processing" or "admin."
  • Vacancy fees. A monthly charge while the home sits empty.
  • Inspection fees. Extra charges for move-in, move-out or routine inspections. Newera inspects every property we manage every six months and documents each visit with photos.
  • Eviction coordination fees. Usually separate from court costs and attorney fees.
  • Early termination fees. What it costs to leave the management agreement if you're unhappy.

A quick way to compare two quotes

Take a realistic year for your home and add up everything. Here's a hypothetical example for a home renting for $1,800 a month:

  • An 8% fee is $144 a month, or $1,728 a year.
  • A 7% fee is $126 a month, or $1,512 a year. That looks cheaper.
  • But if the 7% company adds a 10% markup to $3,000 of repairs that year, plus a $15 monthly technology fee, its true cost is $1,512 + $300 + $180 = $1,992.

The lower percentage ended up costing more. Always compare the total, not the headline rate.

What you should get for the fee

A full-service San Antonio property manager should handle:

  • Marketing the home, showings and tenant screening
  • Lease preparation that follows Texas law, including the required notices
  • Online rent collection and monthly owner statements
  • Maintenance coordination with qualified vendors, and a clear process for urgent repairs
  • Regular inspections with photos
  • Year-end statements for your taxes
  • Handling late rent and, when needed, the eviction process

If a low-priced company leaves several of these out, you'll either pay extra for them or end up doing them yourself.

Is property management worth the cost?

It depends on your time, your distance from the property and how many homes you own. Management tends to pay for itself when:

  • You live out of state or overseas, including military owners who have PCS'd away from Joint Base San Antonio (see Renting Out Your San Antonio-Area Home When You PCS)
  • You own more than one rental and the calls, repairs and paperwork are adding up
  • You've had a bad tenant before and want stronger screening
  • You'd rather not keep up with Texas landlord law. Eviction procedures, for example, changed on January 1, 2026, when Senate Bill 38 took effect.

A good manager also protects your income in ways that are easy to miss: shorter vacancies, fewer late payments, and repairs caught early during inspections instead of after they become expensive.

Frequently asked questions

What is the average property management fee in San Antonio?

Most full-service managers in the San Antonio area charge roughly 8% to 12% of the monthly rent, with a leasing fee on top. Newera's management fee is typically 8%, with portfolio discounts down to as low as 6% for owners with multiple homes.

How much is a leasing fee in San Antonio?

Leasing fees in San Antonio are commonly around 50% of the first month's rent, though some companies charge more. Newera's leasing fee is typically 50% of the first month's rent.

Do property managers charge when the home is vacant?

Some do, through a monthly vacancy fee. Ask every company you interview whether they charge anything while the home is empty, and whether their percentage is based on rent collected or rent due.

Are property management fees tax deductible?

Management fees on a rental property are generally treated as a deductible rental expense. Check with your tax professional about your situation.

Does Newera manage homes outside San Antonio city limits?

Yes. We also manage homes in Schertz, Garden Ridge, New Braunfels, Bulverde, Canyon Lake, Cibolo, Converse, Universal City and Boerne. If your home is elsewhere in the area, reach out and we'll let you know if we can manage it.
